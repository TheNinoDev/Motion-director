# 159 — Active art direction: creative veto before technical editing

The failure of v76 is a **relabeled finished poster** repeated across a full film. Easing, motion curves and successful two-frame audits can all pass while the actual visual story remains bad.

## Actual decision procedure

1. Identify the viewer's central question and propose a **visible event**, not a text heading or feature label. The scene should make sense to someone who cannot read on-screen words.
2. Develop three truly different *spatial staging concepts* for the same truthful product idea. Distinguish subject mechanics and camera logic. Do not average all three into a cluttered mix.
3. In the winning storyboard, specify what exists before a beat, what acts on it, what changes elsewhere as a consequence, what disappears, and what becomes the seed of the next visual situation.
4. Compare staging at the first hook, one middle turn and the resolution with all large title layers invisible. If the same central rectangle occupies most of the film and only its decoration varies, **reject the storyboard**.
5. Render a short real moving rehearsal that crosses a scene boundary. Inspect at 1x, including the actual transition and object layout halfway through; not only polished stills.
6. Review the first full cut with voice/music, then make at least one explicit judgment: `worst repetitive section: [time]; source-level structural repair: [change]`. If there is no visual event without labels, new transitions/SFX won't fix it.

## Common mistaken replacements

Bad: copy card, lock card, CPU card, upload card, logo card, all in the same position with a different heading. Each is individually polished, yet together they are a product brochure slideshow.

Better principle: let something alter the visual world, with an understandable causal reaction, spatial reframing and continuing consequence. A separate item can still be introduced when it changes the story, not just the enumerated feature.

**No fixed scene count, prop count, color-change quota, camera shake, particle quota or compulsory asset import.** Strong minimalist type, honest static proof, silent moments and hard cuts can be intentional. Focus on composition evolution and meaning rather than motion activity.

Contextual Solar/Lucide glyphs may replace an obvious button text label or clarify a control; never elevate icons into the default heroes. User-authorized product marks can belong in the outro. Avoid copying exact visual assets from references.
