# Kinetic atlas and creative polish

The historical references show changes of representation within a shot: a visual field becomes a legible mark, a partial interface becomes a product, or a sparse word becomes a dense page. Do not require the reference files. Use `engine:kinetic` for a perspective particle formation, type contrast, and real screenshot reveal. Combine with `fusion`, `scene3d`, `layers` or actual footage when appropriate. A well-rendered field is not a story on its own.

## Decision rules for a low-capability producer
- Establish concrete problem and demonstrable payoff. If you cannot name a visual cause-and-effect, use an authentic UI interaction instead of abstract CGI. Never open with a brand name unless user explicitly needs branding.
- On a hook, establish one large dominant object or readable phrase. Do not add a miniature disconnected headline over an arbitrary central object. Use empty space only when it creates anticipation or directs the eye.
- Plan 2–3 perceptual scales (whole view, macro detail, close or wide) and vary foreground/background proportions. Avoid identical upper headline + centered screenshot + CTA layout in consecutive shots. Vary typography position and scale deliberately; preserve safe areas and hierarchy.
- Use a single persistent motif and transform its representation. Example: many isolated items → aligned label → actual saved-list proof. Do not use the sample fiction as evidence. A field-to-glyph shot must finish with a readable glyph. Keep it stable for at least a short perceptual hold.
- Use a color change only at a narrative reset, a reveal, or a contrast. Do not layer unrelated neon colors, bloom, scan lines, shake and particles together. One primary motion plus small secondary motion is often sufficient. Use audio cues from actual alignment, not invented beats.
- UI proof: identify an exact ROI in the actual screenshot; reveal its context. Test the start, middle, and end at the posted phone size. If words are tiny, hold or use a dedicated larger crop. Do not redraw real UI inaccurately, simulate clicks that did not happen, or show fictitious metrics.
- Choose text layout for meaning: stack for syntactic sequence, split for contrast, track for repetition/density. Type and proof should be readable on a phone at a glance; use 1–5 brief phrases. Fit text without introducing unwanted wraps. Hold CTA at end.

## Repair before delivery
1. Render a thin preview at 360x640; inspect frame sequence, actual timing and transitions. Mark the three weakest intervals and why. Check actual source/brand and caption accuracy.
2. If the composition is too static, change how the focal object develops (scale/camera/representation), not just add decoration. If too chaotic, reduce particle count, movement, or accents. If type and UI crowd each other, switch layout or crop. If field glyph is unclear, end yaw/pitch at 0 and increase count or decrease spread jitter. If UI proof is blurry, reduce zoom or obtain higher-resolution source.
3. Render revised sections; compare against the previous candidate and keep the better version. Confirm ending still, no unintended clipping, frame count, narration and soundtrack. Review motion, not just contact sheet. Export full resolution only after approval by the agent's actual review. Metrics are not an aesthetic score; disclose if audio/quality review unsupported.
