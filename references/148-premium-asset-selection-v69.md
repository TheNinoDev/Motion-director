# 148 — Premium asset acquisition and visual art direction (Ads Wizard v69)

## Why this exists
Multiple lower-performing agents used sharp same-looking triangles, tiny code cards, repeated rectangles, text slides, and five low-quality placeholders instead of expressive visual worlds. Importing an image is not enough; **art direction, illustrative quality, visual hierarchy, and integration** matter. Compare the actual footage at normal viewing size and the asset contact sheet; reject outputs that merely label generic forms as impressive.

## Asset scouting before building the timeline
1. Identify the real story action and hero subject. Write the physical thing the viewer will recognize in a single glance (e.g., manuscript page, linked key, sealed package). The hero need not be an icon; it may be a photograph, portrait, 3D scene, kinetic lettering, or a symbol when justified.
2. Look for *authorized* professional visuals matching that hero: licensed/open SVG collections, cohesive icon packs, original designer art, permissible purchased stock, previously approved project assets, or custom-drawn illustrations. Check source, exact license terms and any attribution. Independently source third-party icons where allowed. Never silently extract reference-video artwork or product screenshots, even if technically possible.
3. Sketch at least two structurally distinct styleframes, not color swaps: one could be an illustrated dimensional subject; another could be editorial typography with a large tactile symbol. Compare both at actual phone size. Assess legibility, silhouette, hierarchy, material consistency, and emotionally recognizable story.
4. Reject visually mediocre assets. A generic blue shield, flat stock sticker, sharp rectangle or repeated screenshot should not win solely because it was quickest to create. Replace weak assets with higher-quality professionally consistent assets before adding compositing effects.
5. Build purposeful image layers: semantic parts, masks, foreground/midground/background, optical depth, perspective, motivated highlights and interactions. Animate **the subject doing something**, not idle wobble or camera zoom over the finished asset.
6. Connect the subject across the next scene with an authored object handoff or a clear deliberate impact cut. Sound design must follow the contact/transformation rather than wallpaper every cut with the same whoosh.
7. Inspect the finished film at normal speed with real music/voice. Preserve quiet intentional reading holds but fix prolonged meaninglessly static shots, unmotivated hard cuts, flat shadowless objects, and cheap decoration.

## Premium illustration/icon review
- First question: would this work as professional design if the title were hidden? If not, revise.
- Use consistent stroke weights only when appropriate to the chosen art direction. Dimensional style may use mixed weights, soft material light, textures and carefully balanced color variation.
- Avoid icons at 24px in the middle of a 1080x1920 scene. Use clear hero-sized artwork with real animated material properties, not sticker clutter.
- Avoid generic font defaults if they undermine the intended campaign. Fonts and SVGs must have supported licenses; preserve legally required attribution.
- SVGs must be sanitized prior to rendering; no embedded script, remote loads, untrusted foreignObject, or unverified licensing.
- Emoji can work as intentional icons but require a checked font/glyph renderer. Emoji fallback must not turn into tofu boxes or mismatched monochrome outlines.
- The Ads Wizard anime mascot is **for the skill's branding**, not a universal ad asset. The uploaded art is the approved master; do not invent additional character imagery or overwrite it with a low-quality proxy.

## Cross-agent handoff
The asset scout reports candidate, source/license, rendering preview, creative rationale, and a recommended hero. The director compares candidate art on equal-size styleframes and can reject all. The editor integrates only approved assets into actual timeline layers; QA reviews the whole film. Subagents are optional and must be real.
