## Shared visual grammar
- Black or near-black field is a stage, not filler. White type, subdued grays, polished silver; occasional intentional coral, purple, amber or green contrast. Adapt to target brand, do not force black/silver on every project.
- Strong type hierarchy: small setup, bold payoff, tiny metadata and occasional bottom captions. Words frequently accumulate without replacing the entire sentence. Typography has deliberate location and alignment, not generic centered captions throughout.
- Large objects are allowed to extend out of frame. Macro crops, close-to-wide reveals and lighting movement offer scale. One hero at a time.
- UI proof is part of storytelling: cursor typing, claim button, rejection, lists, counts, charts, profile variation. Not random futuristic HUD overlays.
- Repetition is used semantically (many domains, mass requests, repeated rejection), not as a transition shortcut. Background elements at low contrast leave room for the main object.
- Camera/lighting motion and changing emphasis sustain continuity while text may be stable. Hard cuts, matchable composition, crop movement and occasional abrupt contrast changes appear alongside slower sequences. Exact easing curves, blur settings, software and audio synchronization are not verifiable from sampled frames alone.

## Adaptation examples (not source claims)
For a website trailer: hook on one typographic question; demonstrate pain with a compact UI action; transition from cursor or card edge to real product dashboard; visualize one differentiator using motion; give one or two hero CGI shots maximum by default; show evidence; finish with the actual brand and CTA.
