# V75 — Variation without random effects; color is editorial, not decoration

The uploaded film keeps almost the entire visual world dark navy, cyan and a few hot-pink theft strokes. Repeated small code windows / translucent cards / bold white labels create a visually consistent but monotonous campaign. The answer is not rainbow backgrounds, forced genre changes or more particles. Distinguish editorial beats through a *controlled change in emphasis*.

For each segment record visual purpose, composition silhouette, focal position, depth, physical metaphor, material, contrast, color hierarchy and transition anchor. A real story should have development, not identical scenes with swapped headlines. The opening human-made script can have high contrast and focused warmth; violation can feel asymmetric, interrupted, or desaturated; protection can regain ordered structure; payoff can simplify. These are possibilities, not templates.

- Match palette to story emotion while retaining a branded unifying accent; contrast can change through brightness, material, size, negative space and texture without changing hue.
- Avoid over-lighting every line, edge and particle. One intentionally bright layer is more persuasive than five neon wireframes.
- Choose a different **interaction** for each principal beat: contact/displacement, restructuring, controlled flow, release, confirmation. Do not simply repeat code panel + label under a new transition.
- Purposeful stillness is acceptable for legibility and emphasis; accidental stalls and repeated compositions are not.
- Colors must remain legible under compression, small display sizes and common display conditions; check text/controls against the *final composited background*, not only the CSS color tokens.
- Rounded UI polish should support quality but should not force everything into soft rectangles. A visual transformation is the headline, icons are secondary UI polish, and screenshots/reference video footage aren't automatic visual assets.
- Preserve the user's existing voice/song and correct mix; SFX hit the actual contact or new information, not every arbitrary cut.

### Proof at delivery
Give an honest timecoded review: cite actual revised scenes and render; do not call an untested plan 'cinematic'. If near-identical layouts dominate, recompose *one* recurring scene and rerender before final approval. Film-level story and visually meaningful layers outrank any automatically computed pixel-motion score.
