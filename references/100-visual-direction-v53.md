# 100 — Creative direction: specificity and shot construction

Use with 96. This is the missing bridge between a sound script and the existing animation tools. It is not a new renderer, preset library, or claim that every reference aesthetic is already implemented. Read actual supported technique docs as needed. Do not reuse the instructional scripts/designs as finished ads. Original NOIZ-derived observations in the existing reference guides are supporting principles, not permission to copy identity or assets.

## 1. Design a visual idea, not an effect collection
Start with a visual verb plus its consequence: reveal, separate, compress, align, swap, expose, compare, reconcile, assemble, uncover, resolve. Explain what visibly changes and why that change expresses this exact product story. “Show logo with transition, animate headline, show screenshot” is not a mechanism. “The crop boundary moves while the photo stays still; the missing face re-enters the frame” is a mechanism if actual product supports it. Reject a concept if swapping the product name and screenshot makes it fit any app.

Compare at least three distinct treatments internally. A change of color/font is NOT a different treatment. Example options for a fictional crop editor: a macro editorial gag around a bad crop; a metaphor in which a moving window reveals an image; a side-by-side composition of distinct aspect ratios with a visible correction. Each needs genuine proof and a hook that resolves. Pick a treatment for viewer understanding and craft, not tool complexity. Do not force a metaphor if genuine interaction tells the stronger story.

## 2. Plan composition before motion
Use an actual available product asset/state, set a key focal subject and decide the layer roles (foreground, proof, background, support). Sketch or render at least two materially different representative compositions before mass rendering; compare phone-size readability, silhouette, hierarchy, brand fit, and how each enters/exits. Keep the stronger direction. A comparison means actual pictures for visual production, not two textual labels. If rendering not possible yet, clearly present a provisional storyboard instead. Do not treat a contact sheet as motion playback.

Choose type roles separately: expressive hero text, small but legible proof labels, actual captions. Avoid three groups repeating the same narration. Typography can itself be the visual subject when meaningful, not always a centered overlay. Use deliberate aspect ratio, margins, legible contrast, generous breathing room when needed, density when it serves the action. Do not make all scenes navy gradient with white headline. Vary foreground/background relationship, framing and scale with the story, not random changes for their own sake. Use actual fonts available/licensed, compare specific rendered alternatives; no font binaries in deliverables. Guard actual target platform safe areas; avoid clipping critical proof or captions.

## 3. Shot card: work at the level of an observable event
One event may span several phrases; one phrase may have multiple visible events. Before recording or alignment use relative/provisional beats, not invented exact timecodes. After alignment use real timestamps. For each major event write in existing storyboard notes:

- Narrative role and viewer question answered; linked VO phrase or music accent.
- Real asset/state and source; distinction between evidence and illustration.
- Composition at entry, action peak and result: subject/anchor, apparent scale, crop, front/back, text role and contrast. Describe intended screen region clearly; use provisional coordinates if helpful, not universal layout numbers.
- Motion mechanism: what causes what; relevant layer IDs/properties, path/axis, start/peak/end values or relative targets, pacing and intended settle. Choose actual supported primitive/sequence/engine after inspecting capabilities. No invented field names. Direction, timing and magnitude are not adjectives like “cinematic”.
- Secondary motion and sound: what reinforces the main action without fighting for attention; actual SFX event and mix role when needed.
- Handoff into next event: persistent object/shape/text baseline, direction and speed continuity, occlusion/matte/reveal or motivated cut, final readable state. Hold enough for actual proof; intentional silence/stillness allowed.
- Inspection target: exact visual change a person should be able to see, likely failure mode, which native frames and moving interval to check.

A full plan with generic “zoom/fade, cool transition, premium VFX” on every row FAILS even if a validator accepts it. Rewrite until the actual animation can be implemented without guessing what the director intended. Keep plans short enough to execute; do not create thousands of notes instead of rendering.

## 4. Choreograph the connection
Separate primary action (what story changes), support motion (what guides the eye), and ambient detail (what adds texture). Not everything moves at equal speed. Use contrast: fast event followed by a readable result; a pause before the reveal; close image followed by wide context; purposeful hard cut when the meaning changes. Vary easing where physically or graphically meaningful. When one element transforms into the next, match its actual position, silhouette, velocity, scale, color or baseline at the handoff; inspect frames around the boundary. Do not slap identical blur-zoom transitions on every cut. No transition is required just to hide a weak shot idea.

Selective high-end detail: genuine 3D extrusion, bevel and material only where geometry supports the story; use live optical refraction for a purposeful glass hero, not fake glass everywhere; elastic movement should respond to an action (contact or deliberate stylization), with believable recovery, not oscillate endlessly. Use contact simulation where real collision is needed. Compose layered type/objects, controlled lighting, soft shadows and exposure if appropriate. Existing guide 96 indexes the exact technical references (39, 59, 62–71, 75–86); locate supported commands and fields before implementation. CPU fallback is valid if GPU unavailable; inspect the actual render path. Never invent GPU success, feature support, or photorealism. Sometimes a sharp 2D edit is better than cheap 3D. One or two spatial hero moments are often enough; no quota.

## 5. Rescue a weak plan BEFORE rendering the whole film
| Symptom | Change the plan itself |
|---|---|
| Only words change across shots | Replace at least the weak beat with a meaningful physical/graphic state change. Make headline support it instead of duplicating speech. |
| Product screenshot occupies a tiny rectangle | Crop/isolate real state, make evidence large and legible, preserve exact contents. If interaction cannot be verified, use a clearly labeled illustrative mechanism or revise claim. |
| All transitions same fade | Identify shared geometry or deliberate contrast per boundary, direct outgoing and incoming states. Keep only fades that serve a purpose. |
| CGI looks like a stock rounded rectangle | Define why geometry/material matters, choose suitable real shape/mesh/type, assess light and native quality. Remove it if story works better without it. |
| Overdesigned but incomprehensible | Hide secondary motion, mute and inspect narrative, clarify proof and hierarchy before restoring necessary polish. |
| No visual payoff | Return to initial tension or motif in transformed state, with consequence shown. Not another generic CTA screen. |

## 6. Review from the viewer's perspective
Before release, inspect actual hook, proof, transitions and ending at native resolution and realistic phone size. Review real moving picture and audio together if possible. Try a mute check: can you perceive the main state change without narration? This is a design test, not a requirement that all semantic information is in image. Listen-only check: coherent spoken story and intelligible audio, no reading tags. Where capabilities lack playback, record what was inspected (sampled images, decoded frames, waveform) and what remains pending; never claim full audition. Pick a specific defect and fix it with existing tooling; compare before/after, ensure locked content unaffected. Use 97 for review and release. Do not auto-pass based on render success or numeric threshold.
