# V28: optical continuity, shape language and revision gates

Start from the actual locked narration and actual product evidence. The new full FORM cut is a worked example of revising a film, not a style imposed on every client. Keep existing source intact. A weak agent should first write a short visual thesis: what object stands for the problem, what one operation changes it, and how the proof resolves the hook. Every effect must serve that thesis.

## Practical direction
- Opening: use a real, immediately legible situation (conversation, interface, conflict). The oversized background word is environmental typography, not a second message. Use a clear palette and two typographic weights. Avoid microscopic technical labels as focal points.
- Build: give recurring objects distinct appearances and persistent IDs. Use depth and small velocity overshoot on entry, then let the eye settle. Move the same objects toward the next shape. A local glass pass can convey a search, scan, or inspection; keep it off important small print during the read window.
- Escalation: use one deliberately contrasting field, larger type and fewer readable words; retain a recognizable color or shape from the preceding shot. Make the visual action prove the spoken claim, not just decorate it.
- One hero CGI event is enough: sculptural silhouette, controlled highlights, one camera movement; only use CGI if the motif justifies it. Cut to a product screenshot using scale, shape or color continuity. No random CGI badges.
- Proof: maintain a single camera rig over real screenshot(s), not a succession of floating UI templates. Reserve enough time for the actual UI to be read. Crop-resolve labels, never invent actions. If the claim is interaction, capture before/after states of the real interaction.
- Closure: return to the opening composition with a changed state. End with the brand and an honest single action or outcome. Do not add generic filler.

## V28 compositing features
Read `starter/composite_film/README-v28.md`. The compositor accepts local `lens` layers (actual sampling of previously drawn pixels, rounded SDF edge, RGB dispersion, specular rim), gradient layers, outlined type, incoming back easing and cropped premultiplied velocity blur. These are effects, not physical light simulation. Use gradients sparingly and avoid arbitrarily applying every effect. Lens does not rotate or cast shadows; its center and size inherit the group. Translation blur inherits parent translation but does not include rotation or scale trails. Existing full timeline shutter sampling remains available for those motions. Outline is an inner glyph edge, not extruded text. Do not blur readable proof for long durations.

## Revision protocol
1. Render draft plus 3–5 representative points per narrative beat, and several frames before and after transitions.
2. Separate defects: bad timing, visual confusion, bad hierarchy, illegibility, unrelated imagery, unnatural voice or song mismatch. A lack of motion alone is not failure when the audience needs to read.
3. For each of the three weakest beats, choose a specific replacement/repair, not a global FX boost. Check continuity of object positions, incoming velocity, mask direction, scale, palette, and sound accents. Compare the actual before/after output. Do not claim a stronger creative result without viewing it.
4. Only use automatic metrics as flags. A freeze detector cannot determine if an intentional hold is good; bitrate cannot determine art direction. Human or capable visual review remains mandatory. A technical test cannot prove equivalence to any creator.
5. Use a 20–40 second fully narrated proof-of-work for a skill benchmark, not only samples of effects. Preserve original voice and soundtrack rights, lock speech timestamps and align real music cues. Scratch audio is strictly a development placeholder.
