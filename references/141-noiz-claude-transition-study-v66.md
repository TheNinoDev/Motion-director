## Findings the director must apply

1. **GPT-v65 actually improved its graphic clarity** compared to older versions. Bold condensed typography, larger motifs and deliberate color worlds are visible. The problem is now disconnected editing. At ~13.5s blue VM cells hard-jump to orange keys; ~15.6s orange key wiring hard-jumps to cream onboarding; ~19.7s cream toggle abruptly becomes a black static brand card. These have no *obvious graphic or semantic handoff*, and the last cut can look like a dropped scene rather than a climax. Some abruptness is intentional, but the current edit does not make the reasons obvious.
2. **Claude's protection sequence uses transition design in the shot itself.** ~2.9s a red diagonal plate enters the opening and becomes the next scene. ~7.9s a dark diagonal exposes an indigo scene with different depth. ~15.6–15.8s an iris/circular matte originates around the relevant subject to show a cream scene. ~19.5s a deep violet circle becomes the next background. Claude also holds several compositions comparatively still; don't confuse this with a constantly animated style.
3. **NOIZ 1000043457 demonstrates high-impact physical staging.** ~11.5s a metallic letter is invaded/reframed by reflective strips, then becomes a new metallic letter scene. ~17.2s a sculptural structure shifts scale/angle dramatically. ~25.5 and ~31.5s the camera and metallic swarm/architecture form a linked spatial sequence. The polish comes from focus, specular material, scale and camera decisions combined, not from a quota of effects. Its quiet end segment is intentional. Other NOIZ references show very big typography and deliberate black negative space; not every 3D effect needs a clone.
4. **Claude's source-reading ad** takes discrete semantic actions (question → run choices → search → source → proof → pay-off), with a bold neon typographic turn at ~15s. The punch is hierarchy and change in meaning; not every source section needs a matching wipe.
5. **Generic crossfade is not a cure.** A blurred collage of two incompatible scenes is less coherent than a motivated hard cut. A graphic handoff should originate at the scene's principal object/cue and determine B's opening staging.

## Change the next real agent's behavior

- **Before rendering:** arrange the major shots as A/B pairs, write one sentence per transition explaining what crosses the seam or why a deliberate contrast cut is earned. Ask an independent editorial/subagent critic to flag seams where both sides are valid but unrelated. Use the actual packaged editor's masks, motion paths, parent/group operations, camera and keyframe controls if supported; prove the feature with a minimal shot when unsure.
- **During rendering:** export at least one 0.8–1.5s A→B transition test *with both scenes*. Do not accept a single-side animation proof as adequate transition QA. When a scene is restructured, preserve user's locked voice/music, and do not claim unverified product features.
- **Review:** watch at normal speed to see story flow, at half speed to catch abrupt motion and alignment. Use the scene-seam contact strip tool to locate candidate cuts, not as an automated aesthetics score. If no identifiable hero/shape/semantic link and no purposeful contrast, stage a new seam. The best fix might be subtracting the unnecessary transition.
- **Release:** report concrete seam fixes: "changed orange key into cream onboarding via a shared circular access token" is inspectable; "added more smooth effects" is not.
