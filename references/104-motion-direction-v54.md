## 2. A visual treatment is a causal chain
Compare at least three truly different mechanisms for a full new ad: actual product interaction, original meaningful metaphor and editorial contrast are useful categories, not a quota of effects. Pick one governing mechanism based on evidence. Name a visual motif that reappears with changed meaning. Decide how the final frame resolves the opening question. Avoid making every line a title card. A complete ad can be visually complex while having a simple story.

Before full rendering create and compare actual representative styleframes: show different hierarchy/crop or spatial composition, not just color variants. Evaluate on phone scale. A useful pair might be large headline with an isolated proof element versus proof occupying most of frame with small editorial type. Select the one that makes the action obvious. Compare real type faces when available/licensed; don't share font binaries. For every scene define purpose, focal object, support elements, backdrop/material, transition anchor. Retain a common visual family but change shot roles (macro, wide context, action, result, quiet, payoff). Plan enough reading time from actual text and audio; don't impose motion on an intentional hold.

### Bad implementation
Four scenes, each a new large center headline over the same small capture. Add a gradient, a zoom and a transition sound. End logo. It passes basic render checks but provides no visual action with consequence. DO NOT render that plan as a finished ad.

| Proof: matching result | Expose the real filtered result at phone-readable scale. | Payoff | Close on the highlighted result; return to the initial question in changed form with the actual product identity where appropriate. | End the phrase and musical cue deliberately; no automatic logo zoom, no extra unverified claims. |

Optional high-end treatment: a single original dimensional tag shape during escalation, used as a transition into the real flat filter control, with consistent shape/axis. Select one if it expresses the search idea; skip if 3D quality or story is weaker. A real metal object should have good silhouette, bevel, normals and controlled lighting, not a rectangular extrusion by default. Glass should visibly refract a relevant layer; jelly should respond to an action with recovery. Do not add them all. A compelling 2D treatment can beat poor CGI.

### Implementation-level shot specification example
For the realization shot, keep actual list/crop as one source group, and designate a real filter control anchor. Compare native stills at entry, reveal and final. Specify relative progress: entry focuses on the list row, camera pulls back while maintaining the row/corner anchor, then attention transfers to the actual control. The reveal should decelerate into readable state, not stop with a speed jump. Hold until voice points to tag; a pointer arrives only if actual action is represented. Use existing component/gesture/cue functions if appropriate, confirm supported local properties. Recompute actual timing after measured audio. If the group and pointer use distinct coordinate systems, resolve transforms or choose shared local space; never guess hit targets. Compare before/after at the boundary and check actual footage. If original capture does not include the tag state, obtain evidence or replace shot with a qualified illustration rather than fabricate product proof.

Use one main action per moment; secondary movement guides the gaze, ambient movement establishes material. Keep distinct timing roles. Meaningful complexity is coordinated layers and changes, not sheer item count. Hard cuts can be excellent when motivated; choose soft continuity when a shared object or movement needs it. Don't apply identical zoom, chromatic effect, blur or whoosh at every boundary. Avoid intense repeated flashes. Render in native resolution for type/material checks and actual full clip for motion. Technical preview may be lower resolution; don't represent it as master.

## 5. Concrete rejection/repair checks
- **Mute proof:** Can a viewer identify at least the central visible change? If not, enlarge actual content and simplify hierarchy, not add subtitles over a tiny thumbnail.
- **Adjacent beat:** If only the headline changes and the proof stays inert, change the shot's visual function. Not every hold is wrong; evaluate whether it creates anticipation or readable result.
- **Shared anchor:** Is outgoing object geometry, position, direction and speed intentionally connected to the next state? Inspect actual middle frames, not just endpoints. A deliberate cut is a valid alternative.
- **Scale test:** view actual frames on phone scale. If important labels/UI text unreadable, reframe the real detail; inspect native typography.
- **Effect justification:** remove the unnecessary effect mentally. If the meaning and movement are unchanged and layout improves, reduce it. If glass/3D is the proof/metaphor, spend effort on the surface and motion instead of extra effects.
- **Rhythm:** plan shifts in visual density/energy with actual voice and soundtrack, do not chase every transient. Inspect real playback/audio when capable; if only frames and metadata available, disclose incomplete review. Use 97 for defect logs, repair and honest export.

Do not render multiple full films solely to meet an iteration count. Compare representative frames first, repair specific weaknesses and deliver a real film when the task and resources allow. A contact sheet alone is not an animation or a quality pass.
