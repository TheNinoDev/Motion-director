# V34 — elastic materials and low-context recipes

## The short route for a small model
1. Decide whether the event is a real wall contact or a stylized animation. Do not use a collision solver for a logo pulse, or pretend a logo pulse is collision physics.
2. Wall contact: copy `examples/jelly-v34.json`, edit preset, color, velocity, radius and position, keep paths separate. `python starter/contact3d/render.py examples/jelly-v34.json work/jelly`. Inspect `contacts.json` and the picture. Use baked layers for ordinary compositor timeline editing. An explicit body property overrides preset values. Set no preset to preserve legacy behavior.
3. Free elastic shape: `python scripts/elastic_recipe.py work/elastic.json --recipe wobble --preset jelly_soft --geometry rounded_box --material prism`. Then `python scripts/agent_preflight.py work/elastic.json work/render` and execute returned commands. This is a short stylized recipe, not a finished advertisement or contact simulation. Copy the hero layer into the actual ad timeline, shift active interval and all absolute event/track times together, change geometry, color, camera and impact to suit the story. Never make a new renderer for an existing operation.
4. Type: `python scripts/font_library.py pairs metal` (other moods: tech, luxury, playful, editorial, impact). `python scripts/font_library.py list --role rounded_playful --installed` to find available choices. `python scripts/font_library.py board work/board.png --role rounded_playful --text 'ACTUAL WORD' --style Regular`. Resolve a specific available face with `python scripts/font_library.py resolve 'Fredoka' --style Bold --alias hero`; the returned font map can be copied into the plan subject to licensing. Missing installed faces are NOT silently replaced. Inspect actual glyphs, scale, spacing, and license. For metal use wide strong strokes and open counters; do not extrude fine hairlines or whole sentences. Pair one hero typeface and one quieter supporting family. No font binaries bundled.
5. Polish: inspect the first, peak-compression and recovery frames at actual size. Confirm visible contact if relevant, no wall penetration, coherent speed and damping, readable text, no redundant effects. Use `--profile high`/`lossless` for final compositor picture; the wall renderer writes a lossless master by default. Follow the full audio and release workflow in SKILL.md for an actual commercial ad.

## Material behavior
Contact presets: `jelly`, `jelly_soft`, `jelly_firm`, `rubber`, `silicone`, `foam`, `heavy`. They set mass, spring stiffness, wall damping and secondary damped recovery amplitude/frequency/damping. Explicit parameters override defaults. All units are simulation scene units, not calibrated laboratory measurements. The spring solver computes the initial collision, while the secondary ring-down is a deliberately stylized analytical mode scaled by impact speed. Contact shape is a volume-preserving ellipsoid, not a deformable collision mesh. Ring-down may overshoot after release; `compression` may be negative, meaning expansion. No inter-body collisions, gravity, or fluid solver; material preset changes motion, not optical shading. For jelly appearance use a compatible prism/tinted/clear or ceramic volume material deliberately, and light brightly enough to see the shape; do not mistake a glass shader for physical jelly.

Modal presets: `jelly`, `jelly_soft`, `jelly_firm`, `gel`, `rubber`, `silicone`, `foam`, `heavy`. Four editable recipes: bounce, pop, wobble, settle. For user-defined events keep their clock consistent (absolute seconds for solids/compositor, normalized local time for mesh). Mesh-only dent/ripple remains supported by older APIs. The recipe compiler only emits supported solid squash, bend and twist events.
