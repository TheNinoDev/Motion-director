## Sound findings: what analysis can and cannot establish

Both protection videos use largely correlated audio over the same 24.6s. Sample-aligned 8kHz downmix Pearson correlation was approximately 0.91. High-pass transient search yielded closely matching candidate event times, e.g. ~0.24, 0.80, 2.00, 5.28, 7.12, 8.96, 9.44, 10.32, 11.60, 12.88, 15.60, 16.64, 18.40, 19.04, 19.92, 22.08s. These times arise from **whole mixed audio** containing voice and song, not an isolated SFX track. The correlation and matching candidate events **cannot** validate the statement that Claude uses more effects; they show the common bed dominates measurable signals. Differences in overall level, mixing, spectrum and individual effects remain possible. Audition isolated stems or listen critically to the master to make an actual SFX-quality claim.

The user has listened and specifically requests denser, higher-quality, more varied sound accents. Respond by scoring actual visual contacts, narrative transformations and scene handoffs, rendering purposeful stems with the real bundled tool, and A/B auditioning with voice/music at matched loudness. Do not fabricate SFX counts or declare a new mix better based on `ffprobe` alone.

## Creative accept/reject

- **Accept:** noticeable meaningful FX events (physical change, major spatial move, text forming a boundary, layered shape takeover), varied silence/energy, and audible picture contact where appropriate.
- **Reject:** loud effects unrelated to action; static text cards with only synthetic whooshes; identical cue at every cut; the same flat template repeatedly; new film copied from Claude or NOIZ; overloading VO with noise.

The highest-quality editor expresses one visual transformation with its own recognizable sonic footprint, hands a shape/motion/sound to the next shot, and leaves enough room for viewers to understand it.
